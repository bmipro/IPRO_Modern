# Getting Found on Google

Agent-facing help. Written 2026-08-16 as the companion to the platform's built-in SEO —
this is the part only the agent can do, and for a local professional it moves the
needle more than anything we can automate.

## Why this has its own document

For an adviser, an accountant or a mortgage broker, the person searching is usually
nearby and usually types something like "mortgage broker North York". Google answers
that with the **map pack** — three local businesses with stars — before any website
result. Getting into that box is not website SEO. It is a **Google Business Profile**
with reviews, and only the agent can create it, because Google verifies the business
owner, not the website host.

The split of labour:

| Who | What |
|---|---|
| **IPRO, automatic** | Page titles, meta descriptions, canonical URLs, social-share cards, ProfessionalService structured data (with your address, so Google connects site ↔ profile), a sitemap and robots.txt per site, analytics without cookies |
| **You, ~an hour once** | Google Business Profile, reviews, and your per-page descriptions |

## Step 1 — Claim your Google Business Profile (~20 minutes)

1. Go to **business.google.com** and sign in with a Google account you will keep.
2. Add your business name exactly as it appears on your website.
3. Pick the closest category: *Insurance agency*, *Financial planner*, *Accountant*,
   or *Mortgage broker*. You can add a second category later.
4. Enter the address and phone **exactly as they appear on your IPRO site** — Google
   cross-checks them, and our structured data publishes them for exactly this reason.
   If they differ, fix your Profile page in the portal first (My Website → your info).
5. **Website field: your custom domain if you have one, otherwise your
   `yourname.247advisers.com` address.** If you add a custom domain later, come back
   and update this — it takes one minute and keeps everything pointing one way.
6. Verify by the method Google offers (postcard, phone, or video).

## Step 2 — Reviews (ongoing, and worth more than everything else)

Stars in the map pack are the single strongest local signal, and the fairest: you earn
them. Three rules that work:

- **Ask at the moment of thanks.** When a client says "thank you so much", that is the
  ask: "Would you mind putting that in a Google review? It genuinely helps."
- **Send the direct link.** In your Business Profile dashboard, "Ask for reviews"
  gives you a short URL. Put it in your email signature. Clients follow links; they do
  not search for your listing.
- **Reply to every review**, including the awkward ones — replies are public and
  prospects read them as a sample of what working with you is like.

The website Reviews block (Website → Pages → add a Reviews block) can then show your
Google rating on your own site, closing the loop.

## Step 3 — Your page descriptions (~20 minutes, once)

Every page on your site has a **Meta title** and **Meta description** field in the
page editor (My Website → Pages → edit a page). These are the headline and two lines
Google shows for that page. Your starter pages ship with sensible defaults; the pages
worth personalising are Home and Request a Meeting — say *where* you work and *who*
for: "Mortgage renewals and refinancing for families in Durham Region" beats "Welcome
to my website".

## Step 4 — Optional: see yourself the way Google does

**Google Search Console** (search.google.com/search-console) is free and shows which
searches surface your site. Add your site as a URL-prefix property and verify with the
HTML-tag method — paste the tag into support and we will place it, or use the DNS
method if you manage your own domain. Your sitemap is already published at
`your-site/sitemap.xml`; submit that URL once and Google keeps itself updated.

## What NOT to spend money on

Cold emails offering "guaranteed first page on Google" are a genre of spam aimed
specifically at professionals like our subscribers. Nobody can guarantee ranking, the
"SEO audits" they attach describe every website ever built, and the fixes they sell
are the things IPRO already does automatically. A verified profile, honest reviews,
and pages that say plainly who and where you serve outperform all of it.
