# Did You Know

## What It Is

A website block that teases your own Articles and emails the full articles to any visitor who leaves their name and email. The teaser is on the page, the content arrives in the inbox, and the visitor becomes a contact. It is a lead-capture and nurture device, not a tips library the platform supplies.

## Before You Start

Write and **publish** the Articles you want to offer (see the Articles guide). Draft articles are never shown or emailed.

## Add the Block to a Page

1. Go to **My Website** > **Manage Pages** and edit the page (or create one).
2. Add a block of type **Did You Know Teaser**.
3. Under **Which articles?** tick the articles to include. They appear in the order you tick them. If you have no published articles yet, the panel tells you to write one first.
4. Choose the **Teaser layout**: **Auto-arrange** or **Grid**.
5. Save the block and publish the page.

## What a Visitor Sees

Each chosen article shows its title and a short excerpt. Under them is the form: name, email, a consent tick ("I agree to receive these articles by email and understand I can unsubscribe later") and the button **Email Me the Article(s)**.

## What Happens After a Submission

1. The visitor becomes a contact in **Clients** (or their existing record is updated) and a lead appears in **Website Leads**; you get the usual new-lead notification.
2. One email per article is queued. The first goes out about a minute later, the rest a few minutes apart, so a set of four to six articles lands over roughly 15 to 40 minutes rather than all at once.
3. Each email carries one article's full content under the heading "Did You Know?", with the article title as the subject, your address as the reply-to, and an unsubscribe link.
4. Consent is re-checked at send time. A visitor who unsubscribes in between receives nothing further, and anything still queued for them is dropped.

## Track Delivery

Open **Email Activity** and pick the **Did You Know** tab. There is one row per article, showing sent, delivered, opened and failed counts, with a recipient-by-recipient detail behind it. A row reads "Sending" until every queued email for it has gone out or given up.

## Limits

- An article already sent or queued to a contact is not sent to them again.
- At most ten article emails per contact per 24 hours.
- New contacts count toward your package's contact limit; at the limit, nothing is queued.
- Adding the block requires the Newsletters feature in your package.

## A Note on Addresses

The visitor's email is not verified before the articles are sent. The limits above are the safeguard against someone typing in a third party's address.
